A small record you can come back to.

You may remember the first taste. Remembering the texture, the day and the difference between “not interested” and “really enjoyed it” gets harder after a busy week.

A useful log keeps those details together. It can be a notebook, a note on your phone or an app. Choose a format you will actually use; a short entry is enough.

What to put in a first food tracker

Date and meal
When the food was offered. An approximate time is fine for an everyday memory.
Food or ingredients
Name the food. For a mixed meal, note the ingredients you know.
Texture
Describe how it was served, such as mashed or smooth purée.
Amount
Record what you know. “A few tastes” can be more honest than an exact number you did not measure.
Response
A neutral observation: interested, turned away, liked it, or no clear response.
A short note
Anything you want to remember or ask about later.

Use these fields in your own notes. TinyTaste has fields for food, texture, amount, response, date and notes.

First tastes are a story, not a score.

Keep a first entry and later entries for the same food. You might notice that one texture was liked on one day and another was refused. Record the difference without turning it into a fixed label for your baby.

You do not need to fill a calendar or reach a food count. If you miss a day, resume with the next entry you remember. Leave a detail blank when you are unsure.

Keep observations separate from conclusions.

“Turned away after one taste” describes what you saw. “Will never like this food” adds a conclusion that the entry cannot establish. The same principle matters when recording a possible reaction: write down what happened and when.

A tracker does not decide when a baby is ready for solids, which foods or textures are suitable, or whether a reaction is an allergy. Follow individual advice from a qualified healthcare professional for feeding and health concerns.
