Make the next conversation easier.

When you want to discuss something with your baby's healthcare professional, it helps to have a record of the food, the timing and what you actually saw. You do not have to find the right medical words.

Keep taste preferences and possible symptoms distinct. “Liked the taste” and “noticed a change afterwards” describe different things; one does not explain the other.

A simple food and reaction log template

Meal date and time
When the food was offered.
Food details
The food or known ingredients, serving format and any product name you want to remember.
Amount
Your best description, with “approximate” if needed.
Observation
What you saw, in ordinary words. Include where a visible change appeared rather than naming a diagnosis.
Observation time
When you first noticed it. Mark an estimate as an estimate.
Other context
What else was happening, what you did next and any professional advice received.

This is a note-taking template, not a checklist for diagnosing or treating a reaction.

Write what you know. Keep uncertainty visible.

A note such as “redness around the mouth noticed at 12:40; meal began around 12:15” preserves the observation. “This food caused an allergy” states a cause the log cannot establish.

For a mixed meal, list the ingredients you know and say when you do not know them. If another caregiver saw what happened, add their description and name or initials to your own notes.

Do not treat an empty note as proof that a food is safe. It may simply mean nothing was recorded.

What a tracker cannot tell you

A food log cannot diagnose an allergy, grade the medical severity of a reaction or decide whether a food should be offered again. TinyTaste's reaction labels are records you enter, not clinical assessments.

If you are concerned about a possible reaction, contact a qualified healthcare professional. Do not use an app entry to decide whether to reintroduce a suspected food. The NHS guide to food allergies in babies and young children explains symptoms and when to get medical help.

Keep the record close with TinyTaste.

Add a food, texture, amount and response, then use notes for timing and the details a label cannot capture. You can look back at recorded entries before a conversation with your healthcare professional.
